I build software at the boundary between AI systems and the infrastructure they
run on.

For two years at Dell I worked on AI-powered infrastructure tooling: the LLM tool
layer that let storage administrators ask questions in plain language and get
computed answers back, the event-driven pipeline that fed it, and — as Security
Champion — the threat modeling and penetration testing work that kept it
shippable. Before that I studied computer science at NC State, where I spent four
semesters teaching Unix fundamentals to first-year engineers.

## How I work

**I try to find the part that might not work, first.** Most projects have one
assumption that everything else rests on. Building the tractable parts first
feels productive and tells you nothing. The pen test environment and the node
recovery controllers both went that way — the interesting question was never the
happy path.

**I care about the boundary between systems more than the systems themselves.**
The timestamp protocol I designed at Dell existed because a value crossing four
services and an LLM response has four chances to be quietly wrong. Most of the
bugs I have found worth writing down lived in a handoff rather than in a
component.

**I write things down.** The pen test runbook got reused for later cycles; the
threat-modeling skill got adopted across the organization. Both were worth more
than the original task because someone bothered to make them repeatable.

## What I'm looking for

Full-stack or AI engineering work where the AI part is a real system rather than
a wrapper — tool design, agent orchestration, retrieval, evaluation, the
infrastructure underneath. I am most useful on teams that treat security and
correctness as design inputs rather than a final gate.

I am based in Cambridge, MA. The fastest way to reach me is email.
